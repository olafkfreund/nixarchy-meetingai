# Intent: only the owner's voice is labelled as the owner

## Problem

Every line transcribed from the microphone channel is labelled as the
owner's speech, whoever said it:

- **Other people in the room.** A colleague at the desk, someone on the
  phone nearby, or a TV is picked up by the desk microphone and shown as the
  owner. Echo cancellation (#14) only removes what the computer plays; it
  cannot know that a voice in the room is not the owner's.
- **Leftover speaker sound.** The canceller removed all speaker sound in the
  29 October test, but it is not guaranteed in every room, at every volume
  or with every device. What it misses is still labelled as the owner.
- **Notes and recaps inherit it.** "You said …" in a recap, an action item
  given to the owner, or a live answer built on words the owner never said.

The app has no idea what the owner sounds like, so it cannot tell.

## Proposed outcome

- The owner can record a short voice sample once (about 10 seconds, reading
  a sentence the app shows). Nothing is recorded for this without that
  explicit step.
- During a meeting, each stretch of speech on the mic channel is compared
  with the sample on this machine. A stretch that clearly is not the owner
  is not labelled as the owner (see open question 1 for what it becomes).
- Clear cases only: a stretch too short or too mixed to judge stays the
  owner's, so the owner's own words are never lost because of this check.
- The owner can see that the check is on, re-record the sample, and delete
  it; deleting it turns the check off.
- Works with both speech engines (Parakeet and Deepgram): the comparison
  always runs locally, on the audio, not on the engine's text.
- Measured: on test recordings (owner alone, another voice alone, both at
  once) the report shows how many foreign lines were labelled as the owner,
  and how many owner lines were lost, with the check off and on.

## Affected users and systems

- This repo: the mic channel path in `src/speech/session/` and
  `src/speech/stt/`, settings (`src/core/models/settings.rs`), the database
  (the sample, encrypted), the overlay (`shell/`: record, re-record, delete),
  the speaker-embedding model in the Nix packaging (`nix/`).
- Every host with the module; off until the owner records a sample.
- Rewrite #3: the session and speech files are rewrite step 10. See open
  question 4.

## Constraints

- The voice sample is biometric personal data. Recorded only after an
  explicit action, kept only in the encrypted database (never in a plain
  file, never in logs, never sent anywhere), deletable in one step. No
  voiceprints of other people are made or stored.
- Fully local, no new network calls at run time; the model is fetched by
  the flake with a fixed hash, not downloaded by the app.
- Uses the sherpa-onnx library already linked (speaker embeddings exist in
  the current 1.13.8 build); no new inference engine.
- Permissive licences for code and model weights; each model file's licence
  checked before it is packaged.
- Must not delay transcripts noticeably, and must not cost more than a small
  share of one CPU core.
- Tests use generated or recorded files, never the owner's live microphone.

## Open questions

1. **A stretch that is not the owner:** drop it, or keep it labelled as
   someone else ("Room")? Recommended: keep it as "Room", so nothing said
   is lost and recaps do not attribute it to the owner.
2. **How the sample is made:** an explicit "read this sentence" step in the
   overlay, or learned automatically from the first minutes of the owner's
   meetings? Recommended: explicit, because consent is clear and the sample
   is clean.
3. **Default after a sample exists:** check on automatically, or a separate
   switch? Recommended: on once a sample exists, with a switch to pause it.
4. **Order with rewrite #3 step 10:** build this before step 10 (the
   rewrite then includes it) or after (it lands on the rewritten files)?
   Recommended: after step 10, so the clean-room step does not have to
   reproduce new code.
